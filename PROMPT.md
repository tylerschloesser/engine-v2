## What Tyler wants

- **Belts in the reference game**: Factorio's two-lane model (items keep their lane; side-loading onto a straight belt fills the near lane), straight and curved belts, **splitters and undergrounds are required**, spawner and despawner belts for testing. Factorio is the inspiration, not the target: a simpler rule set is expected. Where a Factorio rule is dropped or simplified, the brief says which and why.
- **Compute-efficient above all.** Lots of items, splitter logic and side-loading, balanced against speed: minimal or zero allocation per tick, cheap network streaming, prediction plus resolution. The obvious lever is to move runs of items that share a belt and speed as one unit instead of updating every item every tick; the plan evaluates that against the Factorio-style per-line linked list with a native micro-benchmark at the scale below, and measures before choosing.
- **Engine/game split.** Belts need engine support (replication, motion forecasts and client interpolation, rendering records, prediction). Put as little in the engine as possible while leaving the game as much freedom as possible: for example the game decides belt speeds and which items exist; the engine does not know what an item is.
- **Rendering and motion (Tyler, 2026-10-11).** A belt network spans far beyond the viewport. **The client runs no belt logic**, now or later: that is the repo's existing rule (the client runs no tick rules) and it holds for belts. Smooth 60 fps motion comes from the host **forecasting**: for each visible item or run it sends where it will be over the tick range the client needs for interpolation (the interpolation delay plus a margin), and sends an update only if and when the forecast turns out wrong (an inserted item backs the belt up sooner than forecast, a splitter output blocks). Client interpolation is then simple: follow the forecast. The forecast is inherently imperfect, because exact motion depends on a tree of upstream and downstream state that is too costly to walk every tick; the design chooses a cheap forecast, measures how often it is corrected, and budgets the correction bytes. Culling to the visible rect and the DrawList capacity are the renderer's constraints.
- **Inserters are coming (Tyler, 2026-10-11).** Not in this effort, but items will be added to and removed from the **middle** of a belt by inserters, as in Factorio. The line representation (a run of items must split and merge cheaply), the replication and the forecast scheme must accommodate mid-line insertion and removal without a redesign; every brief that fixes a data layout says how.
- **20 Hz, not 60.** The engine ticks at 20 Hz (`TICK_RATE`, 0006). Factorio's belt rules are written for 60 Hz: in its units a yellow item moves 8/256 tile per tick, item spacing is 64/256 tile. At 20 Hz the same speed is 24/256 per tick (3/8 of a spacing) and an express-speed item moves more than one spacing per tick, so squash, side-load entry and splitter hand-off rules must be re-derived for large steps, and 60 fps rendering interpolates about three frames per tick. The reference game may want several belt speeds.
- **Spawner and despawner**: a spawner is a belt that creates a configured item on a configured lane at a configured rate at its back (input) end, waiting when there is no room; a despawner is a belt that deletes whatever would leave its front (output) end. Items are the existing resources and products (coal, iron, wood, stone, ingot, furnace). Placeholder art: bordered flat squares for items, generated belt art from `games/reference/scripts/gen-assets.mjs`.
- **Decoupled from the furnace** in this effort: belts do not feed or drain furnaces yet.
- **Verification**: parameters are settled empirically where reasoning is weak, with prototypes and automated tests on real browsers (CI's Chromium plus the slow tier's WebKit and Firefox) and on Tyler's phones (iPhone 12 and Pixel 5 on USB, served with `pnpm device:serve`; the `device-check` skill holds the manual checks). Near the end, complex belt scenarios (balancers, loops, mixed speeds, saturated splitters, side-loaded undergrounds) are built and tested.

## Constraints the plan must honour

- Every invariant in root `CLAUDE.md`: determinism (`HashMap` is clippy-denied; sorted `Vec`, `BTreeMap` or an arena), zero allocation per tick and per frame, the prediction rule, the fast-tier test budgets. New engine-crate dependencies need an ADR (0017 §7); the plan should need none.
- Entities are plain data replicated whole-value and scoped to their footprint chunk, and "games must never replicate per-tick progress" stands. A belt line that spans chunks, and a forecast that is a new kind of replicated data with its own correction path, have no home in the delta model today: both need an ADR, and a prediction story (a player's predicted belt placement is a hint; it must not fight the host's forecasts).
- Engine tests use fixture games (`packages/engine/fixtures/`), never the reference game. Belt mechanics are tested natively in Rust first (fast, deterministic, counting-allocator `no_alloc_*` tests), then as `.wasm` goldens, then in the browser.
- Budgets are the ones in `docs/architecture/README.md` and `renderer.md` (client worker frame ≤ 8 ms on a phone, main rAF ≤ 4 ms, GPU ≤ 6 ms; desktop proxies one third). New belt budgets (tick time at a stated scale, bytes per second at a stated churn, DrawList records in view) are rows in `budgets.json` with a benchmark that can trip (0069 §B).
- A change to the game's rules is a dated line in `games/reference/README.md`; a changed default quoted in `docs/architecture/README.md` is Tyler's.
- CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) takes about 20 minutes per push; do not block a milestone on it, check it between milestones (`gh run list --branch main --limit 1`).

## Decided by Tyler (2026-10-11)

Copy these into the draft requirements section as dated lines.

- **Feature set.** Straight belts, curves (Factorio inner/outer lane lengths or equal lengths: measure which the simpler sim affords), side-loading onto a straight belt, undergrounds with one maximum span, splitters that alternate items 50/50 per lane with no priority or filter, one belt speed in the reference game with speed as a per-entity parameter in the engine. Out: splitter priority and filters, inserters (designed for, not built), belt stacking (Factorio 2.0), blueprints, ghosts other than the existing placement ghost, Factorio-exact numbers.
- **Engine surface.** The engine owns one generic transport-line module: lanes, items in runs (`(item: u16, count, gap)` or explicit positions, chosen by measurement), the per-tick advance, hand-off between lines, replication of line state, the short-horizon motion forecasts and their corrections, client interpolation along a forecast, and DrawList emission of items as a game-styled record. The game maps its belt entities onto lines, decides speeds and item ids, and renders the belt tiles themselves. The engine never knows what an item is.
- **Scale and budgets.** 50,000 belt tiles and 250,000 items in one world tick in ≤ 5 ms on desktop (the Pixel 5 factor of `renderer.md` applies), a dense viewport of belts at maximum zoom-out under the frame budgets, and belt replication plus forecast corrections for one subscribed viewport under the soft cap of 16 KB/s while every visible belt is saturated. At this scale the run representation and a compact line encoding are needed from the first design, not retrofitted.

## How the executing session works (write this into its `PROMPT.md`)

- **Phases**, each ending in a tagged, green, pushed commit and a plan revision: research and design (ADRs, the engine/game split, the item representation chosen by a native micro-benchmark); the native belt core in a fixture game with goldens, `no_alloc_*` tests and the scale benchmark; replication, forecasts and their corrections, prediction; rendering and interpolation; reference-game integration with spawner and despawner; splitters and undergrounds; scenario tests, measurement on browsers and phones, budgets; docs and clean-up (architecture docs, nested `CLAUDE.md`, a rule file if belts gain an invariant, this plan folded into ADRs). Decide the order yourself; splitters and undergrounds may move earlier if the line abstraction needs them to be right.
- **Delegation** (0021 §3, 0069 §F): the manager is one Opus or Fable session that reads briefs, delegates, reviews and commits. Complex implementation goes to `general-purpose` sub-agents with `model: opus`; mechanical work (tables, generated constants, doc edits, test scaffolds) to `sonnet` or `haiku`. Research-only work goes to `Explore` or `Plan` agents, which have no write tools. A brief names the files to read, the file to write, the rules that apply and the size of the report. Nothing that exists only in the manager's conversation is relied on: it is written to a brief or a doc first.
- **Commits**: early and often on `main`, no branches; `pnpm format` before the commit gate; push every green commit yourself (never ask); verify CI between milestones. `pnpm test` and `pnpm lint` green are the last exit criterion of every milestone; `pnpm test:slow` at each phase end.
- **Adversarial review** of every milestone, or every small series of commits: the `code-review` skill at `high` on the diff, or a reviewer sub-agent briefed with the brief and ADR 0069, whose findings are fixed or recorded before the next milestone starts. Each new test gets its inject-fail-revert (0069 §1).
- **Shared context is revised aggressively**: an architecture doc, nested `CLAUDE.md` or rule that a commit makes wrong is fixed in that commit; decisions go to ADRs the same day; `PLAN.md` and the briefs are edited whenever a measurement changes the plan.
- **Tyler**: trivial decisions are the session's; non-trivial ones (scope, taste, cost, a rule change, a budget it cannot meet) are batched with a recommended default and asked early. Never hand him an untested step: run every URL, command or phone round end to end first. Memory notes on his machine and phones apply (`cp`/`mv`/`rm` aliases; the Pixel has a PIN and must never be put to sleep; not during his live round).

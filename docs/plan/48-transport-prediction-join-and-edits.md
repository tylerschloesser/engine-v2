## Goal
Replication survives what production does to it: a restored host declares its spawners again, a chunk's version means the same on the host and the replica, a lost frame never leaves a held chunk desynced or panics the bundler, and a degraded connection bundles belt-sized frames without allocating.

## Scope
- **Spawner re-declaration after restore** (found in M47's review). A `set_spawner` schedule lives in the host's tracker only (derived, never encoded), so a restored, upgraded or migrated host has none until the game declares again (`transport/mod.rs` `set_spawner` doc), and every spawner insert routes as an `Arrival` meanwhile. Build the hook (decision below) and have `fx-belts` use it. `belts_restore_keeps_spawner_schedules` (Rust, `BenchHost` on the 1/64 `Mixed` world: save at tick 300, restore into a fresh host, run 60 ticks: `spawner_mismatches` 0, `spawner_declarations_dropped` 0, no `ROUTE_INSERT` crossing after the first restored tick, the restored host's items equal a never-restored host's every tick).
- **Chunk-version drift** (found in M46's review). `Host::tick` stamps every `Scope::Chunk` of every change, including an entity put and gone in one tick, which the replica never sees (it bumps only on an applied delta), so the region hashes differ while the chunk hashes agree and a resume hint over-sends such a chunk. Stamp by a rule both sides can derive (decision below); `region_hash_agrees_after_same_tick_spawn_despawn` (Rust, Loopback, `fx-belts` requests at the origin) and the resume hint keeps the chunk; remove the `O = 200` workaround and its comment in `tests/netcode/belts.test.ts` and the note in `tests/netcode/CLAUDE.md`.
- **A lost frame's held-chunk deltas** (found in M47's `no_alloc` attribution): `Host::seal` clears the change log after every connection's build, and a frame that overflows (`SliceSink::finish` `Err`) returns its entering chunks but never re-sends a held chunk's `ChunkDeltas` of that tick, so every lossy window raises client chunk-hash desyncs (2-16 per window). Fix by the rule the lines already follow (decision below); `lost_frame_never_desyncs_a_held_chunk` (Rust: the lossy windows of `no_alloc_connection`'s `host_and_client_lost_frames_do_not_grow` on both worlds, `DesyncLog` count 0 on every client over 1,024 ticks), and `host_and_client_lost_frames_do_not_grow` stays at 0 B host growth.
- **`Pacing::take_bundle` never panics**: the fit check exempts the first held frame, and `out[..first_len].copy_from_slice` panics when it is longer than `out`. Make it an `Err` the caller handles (the frame stays held; a counter) with a test (`pacing_bundle_first_frame_larger_than_out`), or a checked precondition at the one production call site with the same test proving the check.
- **Bundles of belt-sized frames**: `Pacing::hold` is `Vec::with_capacity(8192)` and grows when a level-4 bundle of 54 KB frames is held (hot-path allocation). Reserve it at attach from the frame buffer size (`Tx`) and the level cap, or bound the hold and emit early; prove it: `no_alloc_connection` gains a degraded window (soft cap forcing level 2 then 4 on the lined world, 512 ticks) asserting 0 B host growth. `host_degraded_bundles_carry_forecasts` (Rust, Loopback with pacing on and a soft cap that forces level 2 and 4 on a saturated-view 1/64 world): every delivered bundle applies in order, items exact on every held line at every delivered tick (the follower's catch-up steps the skipped ticks), `degradeLevel` reaches 4 and recovers.

## Planning decisions
- **Re-declaration is a `Game` callback, not a per-tick read.** `Game::declare_spawners(&dyn WorldRead<Self>, &mut dyn FnMut(LineId, Schedule))` (default: nothing), called by the host once after every `Sim::from_parts` (restore, upgrade, migrate; not genesis, where the game declares on join). The game iterates its own spawner state and declares with the accumulator as saved (the post-step convention of 0079 §19 (a) holds across a save). Rejected: a `TickCx::restored()` flag (a derived read inside `tick`, one more phase flag) and `has_spawner(line)` read every tick (1,090 reads a tick for a case that happens once). The implementer may choose the flag form only if the callback cannot see the state it needs; record why.
- **The stamp rule is connection-independent.** `chunk_versions` is host-global and frames are per connection, so "stamp only what a frame carried" has no meaning. Rule: a chunk's version moves in a tick iff the tick's net effect on the chunk is visible: a tile write; an entity that exists at tick end and was put, moved into or changed in the chunk; an entity gone from the chunk that existed in it at tick start. A put and gone of the same id in one tick is invisible and stamps nothing. The replica already bumps only on applied deltas, which is this rule as seen through frames.
- **A lost frame re-snapshots the held chunks it changed**, the way it sends `Gone` for the lines it named (0078 A8): on `SliceSink::finish` `Err`, every held chunk with a delta in that tick's frame goes to `collapsed` (a `Resnapshot` through the bucket), unless the implementer finds a replay of the lost deltas that allocates nothing and is simpler; the hash test is the judge. Record the cost (a lossy window's extra snapshot bytes) in Deviations.
- **`take_bundle` returns what fits and reports what does not**: never a panic in `host/`.
- **`hold` is sized from `Tx`**: the bench's 54 KB frames at level 4 are the sizing case; whichever bound is chosen, `no_alloc_connection` proves it.

## Deviations

**1. The hook's shape.** `Game::declare_spawners(cx: &RestoredCx<'_, Self>, declare: &mut dyn FnMut(LineId, Schedule))`, default no-op (`game.rs`). The decided `&dyn WorldRead<Self>` cannot see the state the game needs: `WorldRead` enumerates entities only inside a rectangle, and `fx-belts` keeps its spawners on the `"spawner"` active list, so `RestoredCx` wraps the restored `Authority` and offers `world()` (the `WorldRead`) plus `active_len`/`active_at`. Still a callback, not the flag form. The host calls it from `Host::after_from_parts` (`host/mod.rs`) after each of the three `Sim::from_parts` (restore, direct upgrade, migrate), collecting into a one-off `Vec` (not a tick path), then `set_spawner` per declaration. A second thing the brief did not foresee: a declaration arms at the *next* tick's first tracker hook only when the tracker's previous tick was closed (`fresh`), and a restored tracker has never closed one, so the declarations stayed unarmed through the first restored tick and replayed the second from a stale accumulator (393 `spawner_mismatches` over 60 ticks). `Transport::forecast_close_restored` (`transport/mod.rs`) runs the tracker's `end_tick` once after the declarations (no item moves; the declared lines' models are rebuilt, one run list each), so they arm at the first restored tick, as a declaration made after the saved tick's step would have. `fx-belts` declares every `Thing::Spawner`/`Thing::Cycling` on its list with `schedule()` as saved. Only the restore call site is tested (`belts_restore_keeps_spawner_schedules`); the upgrade and migrate sites share `after_from_parts` and have no test of their own.

**2. The stamp rule, as implemented** (`Host::tick`, `host/mod.rs`): `born_from = store.next_entity_id()` before the step (ids are never reused); a tile write stamps its chunk; an `EntityPut`/`EntityGone` of an id born this tick stamps only the scoped chunks the entity overlaps at tick end (`chunk_overlapping`), so a put-and-gone stamps nothing; every other entity write stamps all its scopes (where it was and where it is). Known over-stamp, not fixed (review pass 1): a frame coalesces an entity's ops, so the replica bumps only its tick-start and tick-end footprints, and the host disagrees in two cases: an old entity that moves twice across chunks in one tick (the host stamps the middle chunk), and an old entity moved and then gone in one tick (the host stamps the chunk it moved into; the replica's `Gone` bumps only the old rect). Either makes `region_hash` differ. No `fx-belts` entity moves (spawner, tally, sink and request are fixed), so M48b's scenarios do not meet it. No replica change was needed; its `apply_entity_put` doc now states the rule (fix round 1).

**3. The lost-frame rule and its cost** (`build_frame`'s overflow give-back): every held, non-entering, non-collapsed chunk any chunk-scoped delta of the tick was scoped to goes to `collapsed` and is queued as a `Resnapshot` (`Pacing::lost_resnapshots` counts them; `Host::debug_lost_resnapshots`). This is a superset of what the frame carried for entities (a born-and-gone entity's chunk is re-sent too); simpler than a replay, and the hash test is clean. `collapsed` is now reserved for 160 chunks (was 16). Cost per 1,024-tick lossy window (64 B frame every 32 ticks, the `no_alloc_connection` band), `ChunkSnapshots` bytes built: lined 8,528 B before, 9,201 B after (+673 B; 30 lost frames, 60 re-snapshots); `NO_LINES` 5,344 to 5,892 B (+548 B; 24 lost frames, 48 re-snapshots). `LineStructs` bytes of the same lined window: 28,824 B before, 29,842 B after (+1,018 B): a collapsed chunk's snapshot force-re-puts its anchored lines (`host/lines.rs` `plan`), so a line already on the lost frame's `Gone`/re-put list (0078 A8) can send its list twice. Lined total +1,691 B per window; `NO_LINES` carries no line structures (0 B both ways). Desyncs over the same windows: 9 and 12 before, 0 after. `PanRun.desyncs` read `DesyncLog::len()` (capped at 16, so M47's table's 16 at 2,048 ticks was a cap); it now reads `count()`.

**4. `take_bundle`'s contract** (`host/pacing.rs`): `Result<(usize, u32), BundleTooLarge>`. A first held frame longer than `out` is `Err`: nothing written, every frame stays held, `bundles_too_large` counts it; `build_frame` emits nothing that tick. Never a panic in `host/`. Precondition (now in the doc): `out.len()` is the connection's `Tx`, the size the hold was reserved for; a smaller `out` stalls the connection and grows its hold while it lasts, which only a test varying `out` reaches.

**5. `hold`'s bound.** The emission rule (a hold of `out / 2` or more is due; a due bundle always takes its first frame and every following one that fits) leaves at most one frame over after an emit, so the hold never exceeds two `[len varint][frame]` records: `pacing::hold_bytes(tx) = 2 x (tx + 10)`, reserved at attach by `Pacing::with_hold_for(SIM_TX_BYTES)` at all three slot sites (131,092 B a connection at the 64 KiB `Tx`, was 8,192; the level cap does not enter). The degraded `no_alloc` window found two more first-time high-water marks in `build_frame` once collapses fired inside a measured window: `scratch_snap_bytes` (2,048 B) and the per-frame chunk lists (96 B), now reserved at host creation (`SIM_TX_BYTES` bytes; `CHUNK_SCRATCH = 160` entries, the pacing's held reservation). New `Host::set_conn_bandwidth` and `Pacing::set_cfg` let a test degrade a live connection. Fix round 1: the tail (hold, due rule, bundle) moved unchanged from `build_frame` into `Pacing::hold_or_bundle`, and `pacing_hold_never_exceeds_two_records` feeds it frames of `tx`, `tx/2 + 1` (the smallest due on arrival), `tx/2 - 3` (the largest held below the half-`out` line: a 2,047 B record) and 100 B (so frames bundle), in a fixed LCG order, at levels 2 and 4 for 2,000 ticks: the capacity never changes, `len <= hold_bytes(tx)` after every tick, the high-water mark (`Pacing::hold_high_water`, read after each append, before the bundle takes) is exactly `2 x (tx + 2)` at both levels (a leftover full record plus a new one), and messages / bundles are pinned (1,631 / 368 at level 2, 1,587 / 358 at level 4; fix round 2). Inject (`hold_bytes` halved): `level 2, tick 13: the hold grew`, left 8,212, right 4,106. The bound is now tested, not only argued (the `no_alloc` degraded window's largest message is 12,884 B, far below `out/2`).

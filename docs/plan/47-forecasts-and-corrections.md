## Goal
Items reach the client as forecasts the host corrects only when wrong, in the revised scheme of 0078 A3: a line's **run list** is sent in full only when membership changes in a way the client cannot derive; its **motion state** (`front_stop`, the spawner's schedule and `paused` flag) is corrected by a small **state message** on a halt, a release, a change of the stop ahead, a spawner pause or resume, or a structure change; an item entering a held line from a line the connection does not hold arrives as an **arrival**; sink exits are predicted by the path's terminal `Sink`, spawner inserts by the schedule, hand-offs by the ownership rule. `advance` marks dirty lines by the front-item check and due ticks of A3; the host rebuilds each dirty held line's forecast and sends it in section `Forecasts` (13); the replica keeps one trajectory per item, answers `pos_at(line, item_index, render_t)` by the formula of 0078 §2 with stops derived as `front_stop + SPACING x k`, and runs no belt rule. An engine-level arbiter on hand worlds proves the message rules; M47b proves them on the spike's seven worlds through real frames.

## Planning decisions
- **The engine forecasts the spawner the game runs.** `set_spawner(line, schedule)` tells the tracker the schedule the game's tick rule follows (0078 A3: `rate256`, accumulator phase, cycling, next item); the game keeps running its own spawner through `insert_back`; a mismatch between what the game does and what it declared is a correction the tracker sees as an unpredicted insert (counted: `spawner_mismatches`, expected 0 for `fx-belts` and the reference game). The client-side replay is the **(assumed)** line Tyler has not yet ruled on; if he refuses it, the fallback is a state message per insert (M41 measured 3.7 MB/s on a saturated viewport without it, so the fallback is a different spawner model, not "no forecast").
- **The due-ordered queue, not the scan.** M41's end-of-tick scan over every line was 93 of 168 µs; a binary heap keyed by due tick, reserved from `max_items`, makes the tick O(dirty).
- **Depth 8, no `path` field.** M41: depth 8 left no undecidable line; the client holds every structure it needs to follow `Next`, so the path travels only as `fork_side` where a fork decides within the horizon.
- **Arrivals are per connection** (0078 A6 (0)): the host's dirty tracking is per line; the arrival check is per connection in `build_frame` (an item crossed into a held line from a line this connection does not hold this tick: the host knows both from `LineRoute`).
- **A forecast rides with its structure.** The frame that puts a line's structure (enter, snapshot, resync) carries the line's current forecast too, so a client never holds a structure without a motion state; a deferred put defers its forecast with it (0079 §17 (c)).
- **The engine arbiter is on hand worlds** (the fixture's seven worlds are M47b's): the engine crate cannot depend on `fx-belts`, and one writer per crate.

**What changed from the brief, and why** (each found by the arbiter or forced by exactness across connections):
- *A run list carries a stop per run* (`item u16 · count u16 · pos u32 · stop i32`), and means "the line's whole list at `T`": the client empties the line before it steps `T` and takes the list after. The brief's layout had no stop, so a list could not say what a state cannot (the classifier's reason to send one); and a list that replaces only the `T - 1` items would leave the client stepping a line it is being told is wrong, feeding its crossers on into lines it holds. A replaced line's crossers that tick reach their targets as arrivals.
- *An arrival carries its stop* (the host model's), instead of deriving it `SPACING` behind the line's last `T - 1` item (A3 rule 6). A `Merge` lands mid-line, where that chain is wrong; and with several connections one may hold the source (it follows the item with the source's stop, rule 2) while another holds only the target: one model for every connection needs the stop on the wire. +4 B an arrival with a stop.
- *Arrivals are also how a `Fork` crossing, a hand-off from a line resent that tick and an `insert_back` on a line with no declared spawner reach a client*; no `fork_side` field: the client never decides a fork. A `Merge` landing resends its target's list (the items behind it stop one item further back). Measured cost, not a design limit: a side-load resends a whole list per merged item, a splitter costs an arrival per item; M47b/M52 decide whether the levers (mid-line arrivals with a due segment, a client-side toggle replay) are needed.
- *The spawner schedule is `fx-belts`' rule*, `Schedule { period, acc, kinds, next }` (each tick `acc += 256`; at `acc >= period` an attempt: success `-= period` and the next item, refusal `-= 256`), not `rate256`: the fixture's tick rule is period-based and M47b must declare exactly what it runs. The client's replay attempts each insert by `insert_back`'s landing rule against the tail it holds, so a refusal for room is predicted and only a refusal it cannot see (the item cap) pauses it; `paused`/`resumed` ride a state. Wire: `period varint · acc varint · kinds u16 · next u16 · flags u8`.
- *Budget and overflow.* States and arrivals are unbudgeted (a few bytes each, like `Gone`); a put of a line the client did not hold (first, forced) is priced with its list; a held line whose correction or structure update does not fit is sent `Gone` and put again later (so no client steps a model the host no longer holds); a forced put that does not fit is forgotten (`LineOp::Drop`: the snapshot already dropped it); a frame that overflows loses its records, and the next frame sends `Gone` for every held line. 0078 §7's one-second rule is moot: no forecast is ever left dirty on a client. This changes M46 for a deferred update of a held line (was: the stale structure stays); M46's tests stay green (`forecast_matches_host_under_budget` covers 1,400, 900 and 600 B frames).
- *Structure changes*: `set_out`/`set_speed` are state-eligible (a sink toggle is a `State`, as M41's `LineState2` and M47b's "no run list" need); every other one (create, surgery, redirects, `set_segments`) resends the list. A squashed line, a cut landing that still moves and a full segment pool resend it too. A change of the stop ahead is found lazily, at the first deviation it causes (the front check or a due tick), not propagated.
- *Wire details*: a run count is a varint (a line can hold more than 65,535 runs); a line id above `max_lines` is not rejected (ids are never reused, so a live id exceeds `max_lines` after enough churn): line id 0 is.
- *The tick's messages and log are cleared by the next tick's first hook*, not at `seal`: a store advanced with no host (a native test, a replay) never accumulates them.
- *No ABI export added*: the counters ride `client_line_structures`' JSON (`forecast`), so `ABI_VERSION` stays 41; `engine/test` `forecastCounters(client)` reads them. `corrections` = states plus run lists for lines held before the frame; `forecastSeenLate` counts those for a tick at or before the last render time (from the first render on).
- *`pos_at(line, idx, render_t)`* is `max(pos - speed x (render_t - T), stop)` following held `Next` links; a render time before the forecast tick extrapolates back along the line (M49's to settle with history).

**Review fixes** (fix round 1, from the adversarial review's twelve findings; each test fails without its fix, injects reverted):
1. *A list larger than a frame.* A put or correction whose list exceeds an empty frame's put budget (`out.len() / 4`) carries an `unknown` list (`RunList` flag bit 1, nothing after it): the client holds the structure and no items, ignores states and arrivals for the line, counts `listsUnknown`; the host keeps the line in `LineRoute::unknown`, prices the full list every frame and sends it as a budgeted correction once it fits; such a line is never followed by the client. `forecast_list_larger_than_a_frame_is_unknown` (1,600 tiles, 6,400 alternating items, about 77 KB: no overflow, frames flow, structure held, hashes agree, `listsUnknown` 1; 5,500 items taken, the full list arrives, items equal). 2. *A lost frame drops only what it named.* `LineRoute::lost` (a sorted `Vec<LineId>` reserved for `max_lines`, my choice over a bitset by slot: slots are reused, ids are not) holds the lines whose records the lost frame carried (states, lists, arrivals' targets) plus the targets of those lines' hand-offs that tick (the client stepped the lost tick without them); the next frame sends `Gone` for those only. `LineRoute::commit` is one pass (removals compacted with `retain`, updates in place, inserts merged from the back). `forecast_matches_host_under_budget` runs three worlds at 1,400, 600, 400, 300 and 260 B and asserts an overflow happened, that each loss dropped fewer lines than were held (`lanes`: 7 losses, 7 partial drops) and that the client holds its whole view again within `k` = lines in view + 2 frames of the last overflow (lanes: 33-40 ticks for 60 lines); the arbiter and the two-views test assert "the client holds every line whose chunk set meets its view" at the end.

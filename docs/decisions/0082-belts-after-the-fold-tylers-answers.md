## Context

The belts effort folded at tag `belts-complete` with fifteen questions on their defaults (0081 §B). On 2026-10-13 Tyler answered four of them: splitter inputs take turns 50/50 as Factorio's (question 11); rotating a belt keeps its items (10); rotating an underground turns both ends together (14); the shipped caps stay the recommended ones until the phone read (15, closed the same day by the phone round: 0080's amendment). The first three are behaviour changes to a built game; each replays differently, so each bumped the reference `SCHEMA_VERSION` (0079 §6: old saves refuse to load).

## Decision

### 1. Rotating a belt keeps its items (M59, commit 39576944)
A tile whose line form is a run or a curve before and after the rotation (plain belts, spawners, despawners) goes through the reshape path whatever the direction: the line ids stay, each lane is rescaled to its new length (`floor(pos * new / old)`) and laid along the new direction, lane for lane (lane k stays lane k: left stays left relative to the belt, Factorio's rule). A rescale the engine refuses (`NoRoom`: two items closer than the squash allows) falls back to destroy and create, counted in `belts_destroyed_items()`; play reaches it only after a jammed, squashed pair has been rotated back and forth a few dozen times (each rescale loses about one position). A rotated spawner's schedule restarts at zero credit (0081 §B question 13 stands). The kept ids last only until the join phase: a rotated tile that becomes a run joining the run in front has its items moved into the front line and its own line destroyed, so a line id is not stable across a join. Splitters keep their 180° destroy-and-create. `SCHEMA_VERSION` 11 -> 12. Figures: the rotate-mid-run surgery count 20 -> 18; `edit_during_flow` destroys 82 instead of 104 (the belt's four turns keep 8, 9, 9 and 10 items); `ring_saturated_holds` [0, 9, 9] instead of [8, 15, 15]; the full-game goldens move from tick 760 (the script's rotate step at 753 keeps its ids), `BELT_SCRIPT_HASH` `85fc0c4cfb98e9d0`.

### 2. Rotating an underground flips the pair (M60, commit b4a8db85)
An underground end always turns 180° and swaps entrance and exit; there is no 90° turn for undergrounds any more. A paired end's partner flips in the same action: `apply` reads the derived pairing (the entrance's forward scan of up to five tiles, or its exact mirror backward from an exit: nearest first, the first underground on the axis ends the search, same facing) and puts both ends after every read validates, so an unknown tile in the scan refuses the action on the host and makes it `NotPredictable` on a client (the one place `apply` reads beyond the edited tile and its neighbours: `transport.md`). An unpaired end flips alone. The tick keeps all six line ids of a flipped pair and mirrors each line's items in place (`pos -> len - 1 - pos`, the lane index kept, so items jump to the other side of the belt while their position along the path is exact) on a stack buffer of 21 items (the most a hidden line holds at spacing); the rear item of a squashed pair is destroyed and counted because an insert needs the spacing clear on both sides. A lone flip kills the old entrance's hidden lines (counted) and pairs again. `SCHEMA_VERSION` 12 -> 13. Figures: `edit_during_flow` destroys 16 instead of 82 (the entrance's four turns keep 44, 1, 16 and 5 items; made 2,902 / live 262 / sunk 2,624 at tick 1,023, the extra 34 made measured, not derived: the pair is reversed for 40 ticks per turn instead of unpaired for 60); the random-edit test's paired-entrance ticks 461 -> 660; the goldens and the belt script hash unchanged (the script never rotates an underground).

### 3. Splitter input turn (M61)
The engine's `Output::Fork` gains `partner: Option<LineId>`: the other input line sharing the same `{a, b}` (the reference splitter sets lane k's two inputs as each other's partner, lone first, then paired; `fix_output` re-pairs a lane whose forks no longer match). The link is mutual by contract: `set_out` with a partner requires it to be a Fork on the same targets (lone, or naming this line) and links it back; replacing or destroying a paired fork leaves the partner lone with its turn cleared; decode rejects a one-sided link, a partner on other targets and a pair with both turn bits set. Three rules in `advance`, host-only (a replica clears partners and answers `fork_yield` with `Unknown`; the forecast path is unchanged, Fork crossings arrive as host corrections):
- **The turn.** Per line one encoded bit (bit 1 of the Fork's toggle byte: Fork-free worlds stay byte-identical): "I delivered last". An input holding it waits when its partner has not advanced yet this tick and is due (front below its speed, not a landing across a cut); after any delivery the deliverer takes the bit and the partner loses it. A partner that has already advanced this tick cannot deliver again, so the owing input delivers anyway (room permitting) and takes the turn: no slot is lost. A scratch credit bit (`FORK_AHEAD`, never encoded, cleared by the line's own advance before anything reads it, every cross-line read gated on the partner having advanced this tick) lets an owing input deliver when its landing leaves room for the partner; the partner's same-tick delivery repays it without passing the turn, so shares stay 50/50 when the output has room for two a tick. The room check reads the target as it stands, even when the target has not advanced yet this tick: a rule that counted the target's coming movement (review pass 3) over-estimates when the target's tail is blocked or two landings collapse the cut cap, and then the owing input takes the credit every tick and its partner starves, the one outcome this change exists to prevent. The cost of reading the room as it stands is a lost slot in mixed-speed cycles where the owing input advances before its target (`fork_pair_in_a_cycle_keeps_the_slot`: 375 against 499 deliveries over 500 ticks with the target at 72 and the owing input at 72, and again at 96 / 48; 374 against 417 at 72 / 48 with the partner at 24: the owing input waits for its due partner and is held to the partner's rate, where two lone forks let it use the room the partner leaves on later ticks); the reference game has one belt speed and reads equal figures there. `fork_pair_never_starves_in_a_cycle` pins both inputs delivering under a collapsed cap and under a blocked tail. Review pass 1 found the first form of the credit readable across a save/load in a cycle (3 of 399 seeded worlds diverged); `fork_pair_in_a_cycle_restores_exactly` now compares "advance" with "encode, decode, advance" every tick over 399 seeds, and the per-item reference model does the same on its random worlds.
- **Compression.** A fork hand-off lands at `max(len - o, tail + SPACING)` and is refused only past `len - 1`: the leader rule a `Next` join already enforces. Without it a waiting item landed at its fixed overshoot and the contended lane quantized to one item per three ticks (1/3 instead of 3/8 a tick, 11 % lost); with it two full inputs sharing one output carry exactly what one full input carries (225 deliveries in ticks 100-700 at speed 24; 675 at 72; 900 at 96; per input 113/112, 338/337, 450/450).
- **One output alternation per pair.** After any delivery the partner takes the deliverer's toggle, and pairing copies it: Factorio's per-splitter alternation. Two full inputs with both outputs open pass straight through in lockstep (left input to `a`, right to `b`).

Figures (the pre-M61 value first): `splitter_two_inputs` 36 iron / 0 coal -> 18 / 18, strictly alternating; `balancer_4x4_one_output_blocked` shares [374, 374, 376, 0] of 1,124 -> [282, 282, 280, 280] of 1,124 (3 outputs x 2 lanes x 500 ticks x 3/8 = 1,125, the window's phase taking one); `balancer_4x4_balances` made 2,998 -> 3,000; `edit_during_flow`'s ledger unchanged but one item swapped between outputs 1 and 2; **`balancer_4x4_sub_saturation`: each input now reaches one output of four (200 each), where it reached two before**: at equal rate and phase the pair's deliveries alternate in step with the shared toggle, so each input always meets the same side at both stages; no input starves (the fairness Tyler asked for), the spread under perfect symmetry is a question for him (Consequences). 0079 §23 (c)'s starvation figures are superseded by these. The line record grows 104 -> 112 B (the partner and its cached slot), so the reservations at the recommended caps are 22,020,128 B host / 18,284,560 B replica (0079 §21, 0078 A13, 0080 noted) and the phone pair's 8,298,528 / 6,897,680 B with 90,080 B of the client arena's slack left (`transport_phone_caps_fit_split`). Goldens: the three lone-fork goldens moved in bytes only (the partner field); `golden-fork-pair.json` is new (two full inputs, one output blocked then both open: 135 sunk at tick 400, 360 and 226 at tick 1,000); `mixed-speeds` unchanged; the fixture game's `Op::ForkPair` pairs forks in `prop_conservation` on odd seeds; `full-game.json` moves at 12 checkpoints from tick 1,000 (the splitter passage); the belt field hash `2da20837c13a48b6`; the reference late join 180,448 B instead of 180,256 (the 48 Fork lines held carry the partner, 0080). The balancer's late-join netcode cells: 88,787 items compared, item-exact on every (line, tick) pair; 2,999 arrivals in the window (the phase against the lockstep flow drops one); the bytes cell 1,389.55 B/s (0079 §23 (d)'s 1,392.5 superseded: 27,791 B of forecasts, 2,999 arrivals at 9.27 B), still 8.5 % of 0078 A5's 16,384 B/s. New in that run: the late client receives 36 State corrections where it received none before (32 while the flow builds, 4 inside the 20 s window), read as the host correcting a splitter input's front that waited its turn or landed compressed where the forecast walk, which treats a Fork as a stop only when blocked and cannot see the host-only turn, said otherwise; bytes, never exactness. Deferred (Consequences): a forecast stop for a partnered Fork input if corrections grow in play. The test-only line-structures dump gains a `partner` field with no `ABI_VERSION` bump (additive, test-only). `SCHEMA_VERSION` 13 -> 14.

## Alternatives rejected
- Keeping the defaults: Tyler's call (0081 §B's triggers fired).
- Undergrounds: a 90° turn of both ends (they would leave the same axis and unpair) and a 180° turn without the kind swap (unpaired by the pairing rule) were not options once the pair had to stay paired.
- Fairness in the game rather than the engine: the engine advances lines in its own order; the game cannot interleave two inputs' hand-offs (0076's split).

## Consequences
- Three replays diverge from `belts-complete`'s: every belts save before 2026-10-13 refuses to load (0079 §6), the full-game goldens and the belt script hash moved twice, and 0079 §23 (c) and the sizes in 0078 A13, 0079 §21 and 0080 are superseded here.
- Open, for Tyler: the spread under perfect symmetry (`balancer_4x4_sub_saturation`: one output per input; a tie-break on the shared toggle would be an engine change); on a 390 px phone the palette's second row covers the Export world button and the save line (seen in the M57-palette-look screenshots; the row's own criterion passed after the refusal line moved above the palette); 432 of the field's 12,264 belt records were not drawn at maximum zoom-out on the phone's 390 x 699 viewport (0080, recorded as read, not explained); `M53-item-lead` (his eye) and the Pixel rows stay open.
- A known limit the turn does not touch: a fork that is a cycle's cut line reads its target before the target moves, so with the target backed up by a slow sink it never finds room and delivers nothing, paired or lone (`fork_pair_never_starves_in_a_cycle`'s sink-at-8 case: 62 deliveries, all the partner's). It predates M61 (the cut rule, 0079); a fix would read the target after its move or carry room across ticks. Deferred; trigger: a reference layout that shows it (one belt speed makes it a slow-sink shape only).
- Deferred: the forecast walk does not model the input turn (host-only state), so a contended splitter input draws State corrections (36 in the balancer's late-join run, 4 in its 20 s window; 0 before M61). Trigger: corrections in a played world's byte ledger above the noise of 0078 A9's cell; the fix is a forecast stop at a partnered Fork input (every crossing already arrives as an `Arrival`).
- The contended-fork cost is one extra branch and up to two target reads per fork crossing; the belts tick bench at the gate is the record (`slow_tick_belts_large`, three runs, in the M61 commit message).

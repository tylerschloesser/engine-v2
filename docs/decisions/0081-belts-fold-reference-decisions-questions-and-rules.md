## Decision

### A. The reference game's belt design

**1. `RefEntity` is an enum and belts are tile-addressed free actions (M51).** `RefEntity { Furnace, Belt }`; every entity site matches the variant (`furnace_at` refuses a belt `NoFurnaceHere`, `advance` skips belts). A belt is one tile on `BELT_PROTO` (`NOT_BUILDABLE`), plain data (`dir`, `kind`, a derived `shape`, per-lane `spawn`), never moved: rotation is a put with the same id and anchor, so 0079 §20 (b) holds by construction for 1x1 kinds. `PlaceBelt`/`RotateBelt`/`PickUpBelt` are free (no range, cost or unlock: the README's Placement line) and placement is `can_place` with `BELT_FORBIDS = NOT_BUILDABLE | COLLECTABLE` (no belt on a resource, the README's assumed clause, by trait). Direction is sprites (east, north, south drawn; west = east + `FLIP_X`, the uberquad has no rotation) and belts animate on the item clock (0078 A12) so lane marks and items move together. Caps are declared per site (the game and its tests the recommended caps of 0079 §21; the large-save bench 0; the soak the small caps of 0080's memory row) so each measured budget keeps its meaning; one schema bump per milestone (`SCHEMA_VERSION` 7 → 11 through M51-M55, 12 under `test-hooks`): saves did not survive between belts milestones (0079 §6).

**2. Construction mode is client-local with a tool palette (M51b).** `LOCAL.PLACE_MODE`'s value is the tool (0 off, 1 furnace, 2 belt, 3 spawner, 4 despawner, 5 splitter, 6 underground entrance, 7 exit), `LOCAL.ROTATE_TOOL` and the `R` key turn the direction, touch places by tap-then-Confirm with a footprint-aware Confirm position, a tap on a placed belt opens a popover (`Ui.belt`, kept by tile like the furnace panel and re-checked every frame); a tool or `CLOSE_PANEL` closes either. The headless and DOM drivers agree on `BELT_SCRIPT_HASH` (a belt change re-pins it). The palette wraps on narrow screens (M55 review: seven buttons overflowed a 390 px phone).

**3. The topology is a host-tick rule; `apply` writes only the edited tile's shape (M52).** Rules R1-R8 and C1-C4 live in `games/reference/sim/src/rules/belts/topology.rs`'s module doc. Runs are lines and curves are their own lines (one segment each: a joined line would need a range rescale the engine lacks and holds 16 segments; cutting at every curve keeps every reshape local), one line per lane per straight run, joins and splits by rescale, side-loads `Merge` at 64/192, head-on `DeadEnd`, a run boundary at `MAX_LINE_TILES` (4,096). Pick-up is a two-step put (`removing: true`, the tick unmaps and despawns; a despawn wakes nothing), the client hides a removing belt so it vanishes at the tap. Shape is derived twice (in `apply` for the edited tile, in `tick` for everyone; the tick's put counts). At a full line cap a tile's lanes are mapped whole or not at all (`belts_unmapped`); a rescale refused at a cap falls back to destroy and create (items lost). Items reach lines in tests only through `RefAction::TestInsertItem` behind the `test-hooks` feature (never shipped). Measured on a 1,250-line world: placement 10 surgery ops / 26.1 µs, pick-up 14 / 38.5 µs, rotation 14-19 / 51.2 µs; an idle world of 1,248 lines costs nothing per tick. These figures are why 0079 §14 built no bucketing. Known: a neighbour's shape flips on the client until the host's put; a picked-up tile stays occupied about one round trip; `PlaceBelt`/`RotateBelt` read three neighbours and are `NotPredictable` at the edge of the held chunks; a spawner tile is kept out of `link` (M52 fix round 2).

**4. Spawners and despawners configure engine schedules and write nothing per insert (M53b; 0079 §21).** `SetSpawner { at, lane, item, rate }` is one put of `Belt.spawn[lane]` (`rate` indexes `SPAWN_RATES` [5120, 2560, 1280, 683]; `SPAWN_OFF` clears); the tick re-sets both lane lines' schedules on the spawner's wake and whenever `reform` re-creates its lines (the unchanged lane loses at most one period of credit); a refused `set_spawner` counts in `spawners_unscheduled`. A spawner accepts no input from any side, is always straight and joins no run; a despawner feeds nothing, its lines end in `Output::Sink`, and it joins no run in front. Items draw through `draw_items` with `ITEM_STYLE` (layer 1, before the players; nothing below 20 px a tile). The full-game golden's belt passages end quiescent (a running spawner would break the DOM/headless agreement). Belt item ids are `ItemId + 1` (the engine refuses item 0).

**5. Splitters are eight own lines with one `Fork` per input lane (M54).** `BeltKind::Splitter` is the one 2x1 kind: two prototypes by axis (`SPLITTER_PROTO_EW` 1 x 2, `SPLITTER_PROTO_NS` 2 x 1) picked by `dir`, anchored at the footprint's min corner (the engine's anchor rule), halves left/right along `dir`. Per half and lane an input line (midline to back edge, 128) and an output line (front edge to midline, 128); each input `Fork { a: the left half's output lane k, b: the right half's }` (the README's per-input alternation, not Factorio's shared toggle; `a` the left half, pinned); a belt behind a half feeds it `Next`, anything into a long side or its front dead-ends, a half's output feeds its front tile like a belt's; never joined or rescaled. Rotation turns a placed splitter 180° in place (question 12): the only rotation that never moves a footprint, so 0079 §20 (b) holds and `can_place` has no self-occupancy case; the axis changes only by pick-up and re-place. Art per facing (22-24, west mirrored); the popover sits over the anchor tile on a north/south splitter (cosmetic, open).

**6. Undergrounds** are 0079 §22 (three lines per lane, the hidden one segment-less, pairing derived in the tick). Its capacity per lane is stated there.

**7. Scenarios are text maps with one hash per layout on both sides (M56; 0079 §23).** One two-character token a tile (rows top to bottom, `y` south; `..`; a kind `b`/`s`/`d`/`S`/`u`/`x` then a facing; `--` a splitter's other tile), parsed in the Rust tests (`sim/tests/common/layout.rs`) and in TypeScript (`tests/helpers/layout.ts`, whose `buildPaced` dispatches at the host's rate: bursts of 40, then 20 a second, at most 32 pending), each layout pinned by one hash on both sides so the parsers cannot drift. Conservation is `made = live + sunk + destroyed` with a `debug_assertions` counter of the items the topology destroys (`belts_destroyed_items`; every destroy goes through `kill`). The 4x4 balancer (two splitter stages, one underground crossing) is the scenario that exercised everything; its figures are in 0079 §23.

- **Question 11: splitter input fairness.** Measured on two inputs (M54) and on a 4x4 balancer (M56): one input starves through two splitters by update order with an output blocked, and at equal sub-saturation rates each input reaches only two of four outputs; the figures are 0079 §23 (c). - **Question 12: a placed splitter rotates 180° in place** (built, M54).

- **Question 15: the shipped world's transport caps** (M57): the recommended caps if the iPhone holds `?bench=belts` with no growth and no reload, else the phone pair of 0079 §21. ### C. Deferred items and their triggers

1. **The lagged follower** (brief 49b): Tyler rejects the item lead (question 9).
2. **The dry pass over `Fork` and `Merge` cycles** (brief 56b): a game needs a packed loop through a splitter at full throughput (0079 §23 (a)).
3. **An engine input turn on `Fork`**: Tyler wants Factorio's fairness across inputs (question 11).
4. **A hidden-geometry line kind**: a game must draw the hidden stretch of an underground (0079 §22's rejected alternative, kept as the fallback).
5. **The per-chunk line list on the replica and the geometry lever**: more than about 25,000 items in view on a phone (`draw_items` walks the held lines and `geometry::point` is the frame's cost at that density: 0076 Consequences, M50).
6. **The tile-visual fallback** (0076 Consequences, M50: not needed on desktop): a phone misses the frame budget on `?bench=belts` (0080's frame row carries the projection that makes this the likeliest trigger to fire).
7. **The per-merge cursor** (0079 §16): not needed at reference scale (0079 §23 (b)); a game with thousands of blocked side-loads.
8. **The entity-delta path in the tick bench**: `slow_tick_belts_large`'s frames phase measures structure-only frames since 0079 §21; a bench that gates entity deltas is unbuilt.
9. **The phone rows of M57** (`M57-belts-tick`, `-frame-pacing`, `-memory`, `-android`, `M57-palette-look`): Tyler's round with the iPhone on the cable; each moves a "not run" cell of 0080 to a figure.

### D. Working rules learned (amends nothing in 0069; adds to it)

1. **An implementer never runs the bench or the full suite**; the manager runs them in one notifying background chain at quiet load (the 1-min load under 4 and the 5-min under 5 on the shared machine) and treats every sub-agent "green" as a claim to rerun.
2. **The tick bench runs at every belts gate**, three times, against its record (the path is in `docs/architecture/testing-and-tooling.md`; the full suite deletes `test-results/`); a milestone once shipped +35 % unmeasured (M46).
3. **Two implementers in one tree race every cargo build and the commit gate**; disjoint file sets are necessary, not sufficient: sequence reruns after both report, brief each to retry on the other's mid-edit errors and never to `git stash`.
4. **The commit gate aborts the whole Bash command**: writes and `git commit` go in separate calls; nothing is committed while an implementer edits a crate.
5. **A bound in an assertion is a mask** (0069 §8 restated for scenarios): the one allowed bound is a documented timing ceiling in a `slow_` test that also prints its figure.
6. **A full-suite red on a page the milestone did not touch is confirmed alone at quiet load**, three times, and recorded with its load; one such "flake" (`gc-belts items_in_view`) was a real race in a page's setup and was fixed when it reached CI.
7. **Every brief is rewritten against the as-built code before delegation**, from a read-only survey with `file:line`s; the sketch's premises were wrong in every later milestone (M49 extrapolating backward, M53's accumulator, M55's capacity, M56's "200 tiles a second", M58's fold model).
8. **A Mac-driven phone step needs the cable, not only the pairing**: `devicectl` reports a phone paired over the network as connected while `usbmuxd` (safaridriver, WebDriverAgent) lists nothing; check `system_profiler SPUSBDataType` before planning a round.

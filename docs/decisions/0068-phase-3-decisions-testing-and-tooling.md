# 0068: Phase 3 decisions: testing, CI and tooling

Status: Accepted (2026-10-10). Closes the x86-64 deferral of [0002](0002-determinism-same-wasm-everywhere.md) and the spike B deferral of [0020](0020-testing-strategy.md) §6 (amends both); amends the Context of [0049](0049-compile-budget-45s.md) and [0026](0026-zero-gc-burst-controls-in-slow-tier.md) (CI worker count); records decisions made in Phase 3 (M01 to M39) that no ADR stated. Written in M39b, because the briefs and the deferred ledger these lived in are deleted in Phase 4.

## Context
Phase 3 settled several items that earlier ADRs left deferred and learned a set of rules about reading red runs on a shared Mac and on `ubuntu-latest`. They are grouped here by topic. Numbers are as measured at the time named; budgets themselves are owned by [0062](0062-budgets-as-measured-at-phase-3-exit.md).

## Decision

### Deferrals closed

**1. 0002 x86-64 closure.** Native `scenario_matches_golden`, Node, Bun and Chromium on `ubuntu-latest` (x86-64), plus Linux WebKit and Firefox, all matched the Apple-silicon goldens (first CI milestone, M10). The 0002 "real x86-64 hardware" deferral is closed. A future mismatch is a finding against 0002, never a CI problem: stop, report the first divergent checkpoint, write an ADR before touching any golden.

**2. 0020 spike B closure (SwiftShader on stock `ubuntu-latest`).** Every M09 readback scene and semantic probe passed under SwiftShader with zero mismatch against Apple-silicon (Metal) goldens, so there is no per-adapter-class golden and none is needed. Never widen a tolerance; if one is ever required, add a `<scene>.swiftshader.png`. Only fallback rung 1 was needed: the `chromium-headless-shell` channel. Plain `chromium` (new headless) returned a null adapter; that may have been only a missing `--disable-features=SpareRendererForSitePerProcess` and was never retested. The fast tier ran about 4x and `wasm` about 5x their Mac budgets on the runner, with CPU varying between AMD EPYC 7763 and Xeon 8370C, so a timing change between runs is not itself a regression; `--budget-scale 1000` keeps CI timings non-gating (shown end to end by a deliberate 3.9x-over `unit` run).

### Reading red runs

**3. CI triage rule.** `ubuntu-latest` scatters on real-socket and real-spawn tests. Before calling a red real: `gh run rerun --failed` once on the same commit; use `gh run download <id>` when the log truncates; read the CI run of every pushed commit before the next one. "Passed locally once" is not evidence for a CI-only red; `repeat.mjs` under `--load 10` is (it reproduced the stale-resume race 2 of 8 and the indicator-timer flake 1 in about 45). A flake that vanishes under load may be a window that load closes (the `ResizeObserver` race in a test page failed 2 in 16 quiet runs and 0 under load), so repeat on a quiet machine too. A settle in a probe must wait on the specific event it needs, not on page quiet (`__sliceSettle` waiting for 5 idle frames passed on a fast Mac and never settled on SwiftShader); sequential single-test repeats do not catch this class, only repeat-under-load.

**4. Machine noise on Tyler's shared Mac is not a repo defect.** At 1-minute load near 10 every suite fails on timeouts only; the `unit` suite reads 3.1-3.7 s of its 3 s under load 5-11 and 1.3-1.5 s alone, and `browser` reads 49-51 s of 48 s at load 8-9 against 35-40 s quiet (cause unattributed: startup under `pnpm test`'s own concurrency, see [0048](0048-fast-tier-budgets-dev-loop-and-wire-measurements.md) §2). Chrome for Testing 153 on macOS 27 crashes at about 1 run in 30 (`EXC_GUARD` on `CrBrowserMain`, AppKit accessibility; about 1 in 8 on 2026-09-29/30), signature `Target page, context or browser has been closed`, matched by minute to a fresh `Google Chrome for Testing-*.ips` in `~/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports`. CI cannot hit it. This is a machine watch item: classify by the crash report, do not chase it in code. Loop hygiene: run repeat loops under `caffeinate -d` (headed Chromium hangs when the display sleeps), one gate loop at a time; before a gate `pgrep -f "Google Chrome for Testing"` and `kill -9` orphans (they ignore SIGTERM and hold `gc`'s `--remote-debugging-port` 9334/9337, after which `gc: flat transport parity` fails with `cdp-flat: no page target`); also look for orphaned `vite preview` on 4517/4520 (Playwright reuses it) and orphaned `yes` load generators.

**5. Local macOS SwiftShader cannot present a WebGPU canvas.** Under `ENGINE_GPU=swiftshader` on the Mac, about 15 canvas-presenting fast tests (`anchors`, `canvas: presents`, `mp/*`, `viewport:*`, `hidden_tab_upload...`) go red once `openPage` enforces `uncapturederror` ([0020](0020-testing-strategy.md) §6): the compositor copy to the configured canvas is invalid and the device is lost with reason `unknown`. They pass on CI's Linux SwiftShader. The rule stays; this is a local-only artefact. If CI reddens one, fix the page (an offscreen target on a fallback adapter, as `device-loss` does, with a `local-only` annotation on the canvas-reconfigure proof), never a runner-level allowance. Run the slow tier locally without `ENGINE_GPU`.

**6. Known intermittents: do not chase without new evidence.** Each was seen a handful of times and not reproduced in isolation; a second sighting gets attributed with the artefact kept (`gh run download`). Do not re-open on the first recurrence of: `paced_session_lands_periodic_snapshots` (about 1 in 15 full-suite, `snapKeys >= 3`); `reference_*` full-suite flakes; `[gc] no_ui_change_asserts_ui_ran_and_wrote_nothing`; `terrain: chunks generate, upload and evict` (CI, twice); `trap: gen twice is fatal` (once); `stepping: 1,000 stepTick()` wrong hash (once in 225 runs; diagnostic prints hashes, ticks and req/ack); `plugin-dev` ENOENT on `fx_hash.wasm` (guess: concurrent cargo re-link); `mp/hello-resent-after-pre-welcome-drop` (load-only); `bench.frame_worstcase` `record_count` 65,408 vs 65,536 (setup's final downlink drain is timing-dependent); `walk-ref M34-two-devices`; CI `[gc] ... neg burst sim @slow` and `[gc] sim neg object sim`; `host_admit_path_allocates_zero_bytes_per_action` (red once on CI; diagnostic tail added; no `HashMap` on the host path). Two "not reproduced, diagnostic landed" outcomes were deliberate: `gen.ts`'s yield-free drain was disproven by measurement (`MAX_IN_FLIGHT_PER_WORKER = 2` caps each wake to 2 chunks; `parkWorkers` under a 169-chunk backlog measured 4-6 ms, which is `setTimeout` clamping), and `connected-terrain neg burst` `main` at 111.07-111.74 against 111 is the hardware budget (clean about 103 + 8) used up by sibling burst plus ambient load about 10, not a regression (passes at load 6.6; eight reproduction techniques read 103.3-103.8). A harness worker that crashes after setup never rejects a pending `parkOne`/`send`, so it shows as a `park` timeout: read that first when a timeout comes with evidence the worker died.

### Rebuild time on macOS

**7. The stale-`target/` provenance tax (recurrence of the 0048 problem, different cause).** A `target/` carrying the `com.apple.provenance` xattr makes every freshly linked test binary pay a 5.5 s first launch (one-line edit 327-577 s; `pnpm test unit` 577 s); a fresh `target/` pays 0.2 s (36-40 s total). Recurrence check: `xattr target`, or time `target/debug/deps/<fx_*> --list` on a fresh binary. Fix: `command mv -f target target-old` (or remove it) and rebuild cold (120 s, then about 38 s edits). Do not keep scratch `target-*` directories inside the repo (`context-artifacts.test.mjs` fails with `spawnSync ENOBUFS`). The tmux server, not Terminal, is the parent app, so macOS Developer Tools must list `/opt/homebrew/bin/tmux`; enabling Developer Tools for the terminal removed the per-binary scan share when measured on 2026-09-26.

**8. Reconciling 0048 §3 and 0049.** These are separate causes. [0048](0048-fast-tier-budgets-dev-loop-and-wire-measurements.md) §3 fixed the `.rcgu.o` build-up drift (`split-debuginfo = "packed"`; engine edit 37.6 s, `pnpm measure:rebuild`) and [0049](0049-compile-budget-45s.md) set 45 s on that figure. The provenance xattr (item 7) is a stale-directory effect on top, and it also explains M30b's recurring tax. M24c's attribution, which predates both ADRs: compile and link were about 27-30 s; the rest was macOS's one-time first-execution check on every freshly linked executable (1.25 s first run vs 0.007 s second), paid once per test binary because `cargo test --workspace export_bindings` executes every workspace test binary even when the filter matches none. Consolidating `crates/engine/tests/` from 25 files to 13 binaries (`tests/main.rs` plus `tests/main/*.rs`, guarded by `scripts/lib/engine-test-binary-layout.test.mjs`) cut a `touch lib.rs; pnpm test unit` rebuild from about 210-230 s to about 147 s. Treat the 24c and 0048/0049 figures as two measurement paths; re-time with `pnpm measure:rebuild` on a fresh `target/` before using any as a baseline.

**9. Measured and rejected for the Rust rebuild (Tyler: simplicity beats speed).** `debug = 0` saves about 1 s of 11 s on `fx-puts` and costs backtrace line numbers. Consolidating the remaining 168 fixture test binaries (67 binaries remain in the engine workspace, 43 of them fixtures and `reference-sim`) is worth at most a few seconds once the xattr tax is gone (estimated floor about 100 s for the full-suite rebuild). Restricting `exportBindings` to `--lib` only moves the cost to the next build step (44.8 s then 107.7 s). Running fresh binaries in parallel gains about 2x. The floor (33-40 s against 0020's 30 s) is covered by 0049. The out-of-repo lever (`sudo spctl developer-mode enable-terminal`, then Privacy & Security > Developer Tools) is Tyler's to enable and was not measured with it on; no budget ADR until it is.

### Tooling and harness

**10. Runners are plain Node `.mjs`, `cargo-nextest` installs only in `pnpm setup:tools`.** `scripts/` has zero dependencies and no build step because Node's type stripping still prints an experimental warning that would break the one-line quiet output contract. `cargo-nextest` is installed globally (`~/.cargo/bin`) by `pnpm setup:tools` and never from `pnpm test` or `postinstall`: a multi-minute compile inside a test or install command breaks the output contract, and a per-repo `--root .tools` would make every agent worktree pay it again.

**11. `watchCrate` watches `<crate>/src` recursively and the crate root non-recursively.** Node 22.18's `fs.watch(dir, {recursive: true})` on Linux is a JS walker; a `readdirSync` ENOENT on a churning `target/` becomes an unhandled `'error'` event (the "wasm runner exit 1" flake). An `'error'` listener is undocumented and does not help the synchronous initial walk, so the watch never covers `target/`. recursive `fs.watch` works on Linux and macOS; Windows is unclaimed (no Windows target).

**12. Playwright `dependencies` projects ignore `--grep`.** Using them to keep the heavy packaging specs from overlapping the gc projects made the slow tier run 164 tests instead of 81. The packaging specs run instead as a separate `after: true` leg of the `browser` suite in `scripts/suites.mjs`, at a cost of slow `browser` wall 38-43 s to 58-62 s. Do not "simplify" this back to `dependencies`.

**13. Fixed test ports stay below both ephemeral ranges** (Linux 32768-60999, macOS 49152+). `remote-fade` and `remote-rest-walk` use bases 28_273 and `gc-multiplayer-topology` 28_173; they were 48_xxx and hit `EADDRINUSE` on CI. Ports are fixed rather than random because `zeroGcSuite`'s `path` is baked at file load.

**14. Gc burst controls on CI: 2 workers, not a longer timeout (amends 0026 on CI).** Burst negative controls are expensive because the sampling profiler runs at `SAMPLING_INTERVAL = 1`: about 2,000 small objects per frame cost about 7x a clean window, multiplied by the per-frame cross-thread rendezvous under contention. The control now allocates the same bytes as 4 arrays per frame (about 40 KB/frame per isolate, trip count unchanged). CI runs the `gc`/`gc-reference` projects with 2 workers (`ENGINE_GPU=swiftshader`), not a longer `gcTimeoutMs` and not a changed budget; `walk-*` specs are ignored there (they test a Mac plus iPhone tool). Timing after M39: controls sit 65-83 s under the 90 s timeout (`soak-browser` up to 146.9 s), and the 2-worker cap did not shorten them, so runner contention is not the cause (the earlier "rendezvous starvation" theory is unconfirmed). If CI times out again: first try fewer workers, then profile one worker-isolate burst control on a Linux SwiftShader runner via a `workflow_dispatch` job. Never raise `gcTimeoutMs`.

**15. Soak assertions and the missing live-byte export.** No ABI export returns the arena's live bytes (`arena::high_water_bytes()` is Rust-only). Soak worlds therefore use a deliberately tight `arenaBytes` (8 MiB; live set under 5 MiB; a 4 MiB arena traps) so a leak shows, and the large-save ceiling was found by bisecting for `engine_mem_grows() == 0`. An export for the live peak would sharpen both soaks and the phone check; it was not built and needs an ADR. `soak-browser` does not assert "no GC event" for `main`: its 234 B/frame budget is 2.8 MB per 12,000-frame window, more than V8's young generation, so one scavenge per window is the budget itself; bytes/frame bounds it.

**16. Known absorbed allocations, never fixed.** They stay inside budgets ([0029](0029-zero-gc-software-mode-attribution.md): never widen a budget to hide one): `ManualClock.frame()/advance()` boxes a `HeapNumber` (about 12 B/frame, absorbed by `gc-loop` and the `gc-*` budgets); `gc-sim`'s `main` 28 B/frame from `stepSimTickSync`'s Atomics box; `SimHost.runOneTick` about 2.27 B/frame on a connected sim (`runOneTick` 1360 B, `warm` 1152 B, `resync` 900 B, absorbed by `sim: 17`); the `AtomicsTimer` clock read once per real tick against a per-frame ceiling (0.4-2.2 of 8 B/frame; a slower runner could exceed it); `createBytePump`'s uplink `.slice` per message (priced in `net`'s budget; a resizable-ArrayBuffer zero-copy attempt was reverted because Node's `WebSocket.send()` throws on one); `SimHost` hands `logSink` a `subarray()` per logged tick.

**17. Open question, not decided.** Is `attributedBytesPerFrame` attribution on `main` in software mode still needed now that `software.frames: 600` removed its stated precondition? Dropping it would read raw bytes everywhere and remove the V8-inlining exposure on `main`'s `drive` root. The current design is correct and tested; M36b did not answer it.

**18. Desync-debugging gaps, accepted.** Hash-all is not turned on in the Vite dev server (nothing builds a `WorldConfig` to set `debugHashMode` on the single-player path); there is no browser first-differing-offset line (the harness dumps both encodings to `test-results/desync/`); `assertConverged` is phase-sensitive under degrade (level 4 sends every 4th tick; `rates/degrade-on-soft-cap` and `zoomout/oscillate-48-tiles` opt out of hashing). `engine/test` transitively imports `node:fs`/`node:path` (tree-shaken in browser builds; `no-node-import.test.ts` greps static imports only).

**19. `device-serve/proxy-and-apps` tests are skipped on CI** (`process.env.CI === 'true'`, a deliberate `MARKER_WORDS` entry in `scripts/lib/gate.mjs`) because `buildGame()`'s bindings step runs `cargo test --workspace` and never finishes on a CI runner at any timeout. The real fix is scoping the bindings step narrower than `--workspace`; not done.

## Alternatives rejected
- Widening a golden tolerance or adding a per-adapter golden for SwiftShader: nothing mismatched.
- A longer `gcTimeoutMs` or a changed budget for the CI burst timeouts (item 14).
- Playwright `dependencies` for packaging specs (item 12).
- A runner-level allowance for local SwiftShader canvas errors (item 5).
- Splitting the engine crate or consolidating fixture binaries for rebuild time (items 8, 9; 0049).

## Consequences
- Items 6 and 17 are explicit "do not chase / not decided" records; a new occurrence with an artefact is the trigger to attribute them.
- Item 8 leaves one open measurement (rebuild baseline on a fresh `target/`), and Tyler's Developer Tools lever stays unmeasured.
- Item 19 and item 15 each name a follow-up (narrower bindings step; live-byte export ADR) that nobody has scheduled.

# PLAN

Phase 2 output. The index of Phase 3: milestone order, dependencies and progress. One brief per milestone lives in `docs/plan/<NN>-<slug>.md` (format: `docs/plan/README.md`); the orchestrating session of `PROMPT.md` reads the brief; the implementer sub-agent it delegates to reads the brief and the brief's reading list (`docs/decisions/0025-phase-3-orchestration.md`). Architecture, budgets and the ADR index stay in `PRE-PLAN.md`; decisions stay in `docs/decisions/`.

## How milestones work

- One milestone = one implementer delegation (`.claude/agents/milestone-implementer.md`), accepted by the orchestrator. The orchestrator ticks the box here when every exit criterion in the brief is met and verified by its gate (a criterion that only Tyler can verify is treated like a device check: `PROMPT.md` loop step 5); the implementer records deviations in the brief's **Deviations** section (or a new ADR if a decision changes).
- **The table is in execution order.** Take the first unticked row whose **After** milestones are all ticked. A `b`/`c` suffix is a split made to meet the sizing rule, not a lesser milestone; `10` runs before `09b`, `35b` before `35`, `37b` before `37`. A row that needs Tyler first (a push, a deploy, a login, an answer) is skipped while another row is ready.
- Sizing rule every brief meets: one new subsystem or one vertical cut; at most three packages or crates touched; reading list of `docs/spec/overview.md` plus at most three files; roughly 1,500 lines of new code and tests or fewer; verification that runs in minutes. The rule sizes one implementer's context. An implementer that finds its milestone too big stops at a step boundary (or at the brief's cut line) and reports; the orchestrator splits it (new brief, new row here) rather than letting it overrun.
- **T** = planned on the recommended default of an unanswered question in `docs/plan/questions-for-tyler.md`. **D** = a Tyler-run manual device checklist is attached in `docs/plan/device-checks.md`; device checks never block the next milestone, and a failed check opens a plan edit.
- Markers, each an annotated tag made at the row's `done` commit: `harness-complete` after M04 (zero-allocation and cross-runtime determinism assertions running), `vertical-slice-complete` after M16, `first-playable` after M20b, `multiplayer-in-browser` after M29, `reference-game-complete` after M34c, `phase-3-complete` after M39b. No other tags; everything is on `main`.

## Plan-level decisions

ADRs written during Phase 2 and Phase 3:

- **M16c and M16d were added at M16's and M16c's gates.** M16 left `browser` over budget: 9 of 15 runs failed the 37.5 s line under `--load 10`, and the demotion ladder was exhausted. M16c attributed the time first. That found the zero-GC warm-up to be 83 % of each zero-GC test, and `vertical_slice`'s 5.7 s to be 85-90 % a production defect: ADR 0030's fixed `timeoutMs()` means any producer waking the sim faster than its tick interval stops ticks until a gap opens. M16d fixes that before M16b, because every real client renders continuously.
- **M15e was added at M15d's gate, and it corrects a diagnosis rather than acting on one.** M15b's push left `poll_skips_a_spurious_tick_on_a_ring_wake` red on `ubuntu-latest` (79 and 83 ticks against a ceiling of 40.5) and green on every local run. It was recorded in `PROMPT.md` as a real spurious-tick defect, on the reasoning that "a slow runner would run *fewer* ticks, not more". Measuring it before delegating inverted that: `SimHostCounters.ticksRun` is cumulative from `simHost.start()`, the test compares it against a *poke-window* expectation of 30, and inserting idle time before the poke adds exactly one tick per 50 ms (26 → 48 → 86 at 0 / 1000 / 3000 ms, macOS/arm64, `efde638`). A slow runner spends longer between worker setup and the first poke, so it accumulates more. The test's measurement is invalid; `worker/sim.ts`, `atomics-timer.ts` and ADR 0030 are not implicated. M15e repairs the measurement and must prove the repaired test still fails with the guard reverted, since proving that is the test's whole purpose.
- **M15d was added at M15b's gate too, acting on `questions-for-tyler.md` Q13's recommended default.** M13b recorded `client.ts`'s per-frame `performance.now()` as costing ~11.96 B/frame on `main` in the interpreter tier and deliberately left it, absorbed by every page's `main` budget. Three measurements turned that into a blocker: 1 failure in 6 under `--load 10` at M15's gate (same rate on M15's *base* commit, so pre-existing), **7 in 15 with no injected load** once M15b grew the suite from 98 to 103 tests, and M15c will add another zero-GC page. A gate red half the time certifies nothing. **The remedy cannot be a budget** — ADR 0029 makes a budget that stops a control tripping the exact failure mode to avoid, and M13b held `main` at 30 for that reason — so M15d applies M13b's own fix to the isolate M13b did not touch, and re-derives the absorbing budgets *downward*. It runs before M15c: order in this table is execution order.
- **M15c was added at M15b's gate, not planned in Phase 2.** Building M15b's last two deliverables found a real bug in pre-existing M07/M08b code: `Cache::evict_if_present` — the path `TerrainStore::replace_overlay` and `clear_overlay` take — frees the slot without calling `push_event`, and `GenQueue::set_view` returns early on an unchanged `view.visible` without consulting any cache event. So a chunk the client pristine-generated, then received a host snapshot for, is correctly evicted and then never regenerated while the camera holds still. Both halves were verified in source at the gate. It could not have been caught earlier: no code before M15b's `game_instance.rs` restructuring ever had a `Replica` and a `TerrainFeed` sharing one `TerrainStore`. M15b is ticked for what it landed (ring `Connection`, worker plumbing, ABI 8 → 9, connected-path tests); `overlay_tile_reaches_screen` and the zero-GC panning window moved to M15c, and **M16's After moved from 15b to 15c**, because "chunked world on screen" is part of the vertical slice its marker claims.

- `docs/decisions/0028-zero-gc-two-measured-windows.md`: at M11's gate the new `input` page failed its `client` isolate deterministically (15/15). Four fix rounds established that the ~13.5 KB lump is a **one-off V8 JIT code-installation burst**, billed to whichever JS frame happens to be executing (seven were observed), not `Atomics.wait` bookkeeping and not engine allocation. Assertion B now takes the lower of two consecutive 600-frame windows: a one-off lands in at most one, per-frame allocation lands in both. Nothing is excluded by name. Supersedes 0027; amends 0016 §3 step 7. Its M11 amendment re-derives every page's `main` budget downward under the corrected instrument (`gc-loop` 45, `topology` 42, `echo` 30, `gen` 42, `terrain` 110, `input` 190) and restores the `object` control to one object; the strict worker figure stays 8.
- `docs/decisions/0027-zero-gc-excludes-blocking-primitive-bookkeeping.md`: **superseded by 0028** before it ever held. It excluded the same bytes by the frame name `waitForWake`, on the premise that they were V8's bookkeeping for a genuinely-blocking `Atomics.wait`. The premise was false and the exclusion missed whenever V8 billed the lump to another frame. Kept, not rewritten, because 0028's reasoning only makes sense against it.
- `docs/decisions/0029-zero-gc-software-mode-attribution.md`: at M10's gate, turning on `GC_MODE=software` in CI ran 0016 caveat b's attribution mechanism for the first time ever and it did not work: the negative-control hooks fired as *siblings* of their named attribution roots, so every worker control and `main`'s control on all five production pages attributed 0 while the raw counters saw the allocation. The hooks now nest inside the root (never widen a root to reach a control); and because V8 inlined `body()` away on one isolate 5/5 (0.0267 B/frame attributed against 16 real), software mode attributes **only `main`** — every other isolate uses raw bytes in both modes. Records the correction that 0016's stated rationale for attribution was arithmetic (small N), a precondition `software.frames: 600` removes for every isolate: `main` keeps attribution as a live inlining detector, a new reason, not a borrowed one. Amends 0016 §3 caveat b. Hardware mode was never affected.
- **M13b was added at M14's gate, not planned in Phase 2.** Reading CI on M14's `done` commit found `gc-sim`'s negative controls intermittently red. Attribution from a CI artefact named `SimHost.runOneTickTimed`'s two per-tick `services.clock.now()` reads: `performance.now()` returns a fractional double that V8 boxes into a `HeapNumber` in the interpreter tier, 11.92 B per read, 23.84 B/tick against a strict worker budget of 8. It is intermittent because a busy sibling isolate plausibly starves `sim`'s TurboFan job, so the measured window runs interpreted — a zero-allocation guarantee that holds only when V8 wins a compilation race is not a guarantee (0016 §1). `onFire`, the production pacing path, has the same read and no zero-GC page exercises it, so M13b builds that page first. Two other defects found in the same gate were fixed in place: `armedLoop`'s per-call message literals (`d602f44`) and the missing `windowByFn` in every zero-GC failure message (`2024709`), which is what made all three findable.
- `docs/decisions/0030-sim-host-resync-based-pacing.md`: `SimHost` reads the wall clock only once every `RESYNC_TICKS` (8) ticks. `performance.now()` returns a fractional double that V8 boxes into a `HeapNumber` in the interpreter tier — ~11.92 B per read, so even one read per tick exceeds the strict worker budget of 8, and `Math.trunc()` does not help because the box happens at the native call's return. M13's four reads per wake (two in `runOneTickTimed`, one in `onFire`'s due check, one in `AtomicsTimer.timeoutMs`) are replaced by integer-only arithmetic between periodic resyncs. Measured rejections, so they are not re-litigated: a WASM-imported clock still allocated 7.98 B/tick (its glue is ordinary JS), and waiting a constant period without a clock paces at period + work time, which drifts without bound. `tickOverruns` now detects an overrun per resync window rather than per tick, and `ticksDropped` derives from the resync delta against the catch-up cap; drift is bounded at one window and self-correcting. Amends M13's pacing decision. **`AtomicsTimer.poll()` now fires unconditionally on every wake** — correct only while nothing but the timer wakes a production sim worker, flagged in M15's brief.
- `docs/decisions/0031-browser-suite-five-workers.md`: the `browser` suite runs 5 Playwright workers, not 3, measured at M16c on Tyler's 14-logical-CPU Mac as faster both quiet and under `--load 10`, with no new failure or `parkWorkers` timeout. Amends 0020 §3.
- `docs/decisions/0032-atomics-timer-bounds-external-wakes.md`: `AtomicsTimer` keeps a proven and an estimated bound on elapsed time; `timeoutMs()` answers the time left by the estimate, and the clock is read only when the two bounds disagree about the deadline, which never happens while nothing interrupts a wait. It fixes M16c's finding that any producer waking the sim faster than its tick interval stopped ticks entirely (0 ticks in 2 s at 60 Hz wakes on base). A clock-free candidate was measured and rejected: Chrome overshoots small `Atomics.wait` timeouts, so credited time ran at about 60 % of real time. Amends 0030 §2.
- `docs/decisions/0035-clientside-on-init.md`: `ClientSide` gains `on_init(&mut self, seed, &Params)`, called once by `ClientInstance::init` right after `Default::default()`, before `frame`/`extract`/`ui` ever run — the one channel a client can learn the seed/params its own world was created with (`Default` itself takes no arguments). Default no-op; every existing game/fixture unaffected. Fixes M20b's gate finding that `RefClient`'s spawn rule computed from a hard-coded `content::SEED` duplicate, wrong for any other seed. Amends 0003's `ClientSide` trait block.
- `docs/decisions/0026-zero-gc-burst-controls-in-slow-tier.md`: at M09's gate the `browser` suite trip-wire fired (24 s of 25 s). WebKit and Firefox repeats moved to the slow-tier `engines` leg (0020 §4, first rung), and generated `burst` negative controls are `@slow` for every zero-GC page but `gc-loop`, replaced in the fast tier by a discovered-isolates assertion in each clean test. `pnpm test` no longer proves three-browser determinism; `pnpm test:slow` and M10's CI do. Amends 0016 §3.8.
